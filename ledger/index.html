01How it posts

Every module posts through one route.

Orbit and Pulse never write a journal of their own. They tell Ledger what happened, and Ledger decides the accounts, the period and the audit trail.

Posted once

Every document arrives with a posting key unique to it. A document sent twice is posted once.

The right accounts, set once

Each module names a transaction type. Your company's account mappings turn it into real accounts, so people keying stock or payroll never need the chart of accounts.

Control accounts that agree

Supplier balances and the payables control account are written together in one transaction, so the control account always equals the sum of supplier balances.

A closed month stays closed

A posting into a closed period is refused when it is made, not caught later in review.

03What it carries

The books a growing business keeps.

One general ledger, with the accounts, payments and controls around it. Supplier and customer accounts are kept in the currency of each document and in your base currency.

GL

General ledger

Chart of accounts with groups, control-account rules and cash-flow classification. Manual journals with approval and reversal, and a posted ledger that is never edited.

AP·AR

Supplier and customer accounts

Payables and receivables fed by purchasing, sales and payroll. Open items, ageing, settlement state and the balance for every party.

PAY

Payments and receipts

Supplier payments and customer receipts keyed, approved and posted, then allocated against open invoices, debit notes and credit notes at each document's own rate.

ADV

Advances on account

A receipt with nothing to allocate is held on account and applied to later invoices without a second journal.

PDC

Post-dated cheques

A register for cheques received and issued: held, banked, realised, dishonoured with the bank charge, or replaced. Held cheques are cover, never settlement.

BATCH

Payment batches

Net pay and EPF, ETF and APIT remittances arrive from Pulse as batches. Ledger approves them, produces the bank's payment file and records what the bank paid, payment by payment.

PER

Period control

Financial years and periods for each company, opened and closed for each subledger. A posting into a closed period is refused.

FS

Statements

Trial balance, profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow and the general ledger enquiry, by period, branch and cost centre, to Excel or print.

TAX

Tax and currency

Effective-dated tax codes, so a rate change never rewrites history. Documents carry currency, rate and both amounts; settling at another rate posts the difference.

04Post-dated cheques

A held cheque is cover, not payment.

Cheques received and issued sit in their own register. Nothing is settled while a cheque is held, so an invoice with a cheque against it stays open until the cheque is realised.

Coming next in the report pack: payables and receivables ageing with a covered-by-cheque column, so you can see what is due and what is already backed by a cheque in hand.

05Controls

Controls that are not optional.

For the people who sign the accounts and the people who audit them. These are built into how Ledger works, not settings someone can forget.

Who may do what

  • Two gates on every screen. The capability must be switched on for your company, and the person must hold the permission.
  • Permissions by action: view, create, edit, delete, approve, post, reverse, process, export and print.
  • Segregation of duties. Keying, approving and posting a payment are separate permissions, and role templates keep them apart.
  • Company and branch scope on every query, so nothing crosses a company boundary by accident.

What the system remembers

  • An audit entry for every create, submit, approve, post, reverse, cancel and export, with the field values that changed.
  • Posted journals are never edited. A correction is a reversal and a new journal, both dated and both with a reason.
  • Reasons are required for a reversal, a cancellation, abandoning a payment and reopening a period.
  • Numbering per company and document type, with gaps allowed only where the law permits them.

10Questions

The details worth checking.

Tell us where your process differs. We confirm what the current release supports before anything is agreed.

Can we use Ledger without Orbit or Pulse?

Yes. Ledger is sold on its own, with its chart of accounts, journals, payments, receipts, cheques and statements. Supplier and customer invoices reach Ledger from the product that raises them, so if you invoice in another system today, tell us how. We confirm what the current release supports before anything is agreed.

We already use Orbit or Pulse. Do we have to add Ledger?

No. Ledger is a separate product, and adding it is your choice. If you add it later, we agree your opening balances and how your existing records should post before it is switched on.

Can a posted journal be changed?

No. Posted journals are never edited. A correction is a reversal and a new journal, both dated and both with a reason, so the original stays in view.

What happens if someone posts into a month we have closed?

The posting is refused when it is made, rather than found later in review. Reopening a period needs a reason, and the reason is kept in the audit trail.

Which banks can Ledger produce payment files for?

A generic CSV file is included. A bank's own layout is added as a new format without changing how batches work. Tell us which bank and account you pay from, and we confirm the format before anything is agreed.

Does Ledger handle foreign currency?

Yes, when foreign-currency transactions are switched on for your company. Each document carries its currency, its rate and both amounts, and a settlement at a different rate posts the exchange difference.

Does Ledger file our tax returns?

No. Ledger records tax by effective-dated tax code, so a rate change does not rewrite history. Returns, filing and tax advice stay with your accountant or tax adviser.

How much will it cost?

Ledger is quoted on its own, or together with Orbit and Pulse, in a written proposal. We do not publish a figure on this page.
